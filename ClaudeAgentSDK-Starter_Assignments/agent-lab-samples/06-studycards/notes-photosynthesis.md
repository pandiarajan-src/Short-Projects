# Lecture 7: Photosynthesis

## 1. What it is

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae and some bacteria use to turn light energy into chemical energy.
The chemical energy is stored in glucose, a simple sugar.
Oxygen is released as a by-product.

The overall equation is:
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2

In words: carbon dioxide and water, using light, make glucose and oxygen.

## 2. Where it happens

Photosynthesis takes place in the chloroplasts of plant cells.
Most chloroplasts are found in the mesophyll cells of the leaf.
Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll.
Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

Inside a chloroplast:
- Thylakoids are flattened membrane sacs where the light-dependent reactions happen.
- A stack of thylakoids is called a granum (plural: grana).
- The stroma is the fluid around the thylakoids, where the Calvin cycle happens.

## 3. Gas exchange

Carbon dioxide enters the leaf through small pores called stomata.
Oxygen and water vapour leave through the same stomata.
Guard cells open and close the stomata.
On hot, dry days, stomata close to save water, which slows photosynthesis.

## 4. The two stages

### 4a. Light-dependent reactions

- Happen in the thylakoid membranes.
- Need light directly.
- Water is split (photolysis), releasing oxygen.
- Produce ATP and NADPH, which carry energy to the next stage.

### 4b. Light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle)

- Happen in the stroma.
- Do not need light directly, but need the ATP and NADPH from stage one.
- Carbon dioxide is fixed into organic molecules.
- The enzyme RuBisCO catalyses the first step of carbon fixation.
- RuBisCO is thought to be the most abundant protein on Earth.
- The cycle produces G3P, which the plant uses to build glucose.

## 5. Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis depends on three main limiting factors:
1. Light intensity - more light increases the rate, until another factor limits it.
2. Carbon dioxide concentration - more CO2 increases the rate, up to a point.
3. Temperature - enzymes work fastest at an optimum temperature; too hot and they denature.

Whichever factor is in shortest supply limits the overall rate.

## 6. Why it matters

- Photosynthesis is the source of almost all the oxygen in the atmosphere.
- It is the starting point of most food chains.
- Fossil fuels store energy that was originally captured by photosynthesis millions of years ago.

## 7. Recap (repeated from earlier sections)

- Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplasts.
- Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs light.
- CO2 enters through the stomata.
- The light-dependent reactions happen in the thylakoids; the Calvin cycle happens in the stroma.
- Oxygen comes from splitting water, not from carbon dioxide.
